One formula joins smooth time and computer time

What you're looking at: Left: a map of every way a spring can move. Right-to-left says how fast the motion dies away; up-down says how fast it wobbles. The two brass knobs are this spring's own motion. Right: the same map as a computer sees it, taking a snapshot every T seconds.

Why it matters: Every digital filter, audio plug-in and robot controller is a smooth design moved onto a computer. The bridge is z = esT. The glowing line of never-fading wobbles becomes the circle; motions that die away land inside it; motions that blow up land outside. And because a circle repeats, wobbles that are too fast land on top of slow ones. That's aliasing. (Engineers call the left map the Laplace s-plane and the right one the z-plane.)

How to use it

  1. Press Bend into the z-plane and watch the flat map roll up into the disc.
  2. Drag a brass knob left or right: the spring rings longer or shorter, and its twin moves in or out on the disc.
  3. Drag a knob past the glowing line: the spring blows up just as its twin leaves the circle.
  4. Slide Sample slower: the green snapshots drift onto a slower fake wave when the wobble is too fast to see.
  5. Reset to example puts everything back.
